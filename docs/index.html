Referees

How this works, and what the terms mean
Prediction
The model's estimate for a match: expected corners for each side, and the chance of each market. Every match gets one.
Call
Landed / missed
Whether a call came off once the result was in. A call is made together with its prediction, against the corners line of the day, and both freeze at kick-off. Changing a threshold later never rewrites a past call.
Chance
How likely the model thinks something is. 60% means it should happen about 6 times in 10.
Fair odds
The chance written as decimal odds (1 ÷ chance). 60% is 1.67. A price higher than this would be good value if the model is right.
Corner dominance
Big gap
High corners
A side the model expects to take at least the chosen number of corners (5+, 6+ or 7+; switch it on the High-corner sides panel). 6+ is the default. Over six seasons about 1 side in 8 was tagged 6+, and 60% of them reached it, against 40% of all sides. At 5+ half of all sides qualify, so the tag picks out much less. A prediction, not a call.
Under 3 corners
Line
xG
Expected goals, from the quality of chances created. The model blends each side's xG with the goals they actually scored.
Warning
Something in a side's recent matches the model can't fully account for: a red card, a freak corner count (1 in 33 or rarer), a 4+ goal margin, or a sharp three-match trend that could mean a change of manager, system or injuries. Warnings don't change the numbers; they tell you when to trust them less.
Cards
Yellow and red cards shown to each side (a red counts as one card). Predicted from each side's card and foul record, how often its opponents get booked, and the referee. The two sides' cards rise and fall together, because heated matches get both sides booked.
Referee: strict or lenient
How many cards a referee shows compared with the league average, from every match they've refereed since 2017-18, with recent matches counting more. "Strict" is 10% or more above average, "lenient" 10% or more below. Tested on seasons the ratings hadn't seen, the strictest quarter of referees saw over 3.5 cards in 57% of matches and the most lenient quarter in 42%. Appointments are published about a day before the match; until then a match assumes an average referee.
Clean sheet
BTTS
Both teams score.
Gameweek
Matches played within a few days of each other, with no club playing twice. Worked out from the dates, so Thursday–Monday and Tuesday–Wednesday rounds both group correctly.

Where the model is strongest. Telling which side will dominate the corner count: in big-gap matches over six seasons, the side expected to win more corners did so 72% of the time. Corner totals are harder. Goals calls are well calibrated: over six seasons the model said 62% on average and 62% landed.

How corners are predicted. Team ratings for corners won and conceded, and for shots, each with a home and away adjustment. Recent matches count more. Promoted and relegated clubs start from how clubs arriving the same way have done since 2017. Settings are tuned on four past seasons and checked on two others. The match total is predicted first and then split between the sides, because one side's corners come partly at the other's expense. Match statistics only: no bookmaker prices go in.

How goals are predicted. The same rating method applied to a blend of goals, xG and shots on target, pulled harder towards average because goals are noisier. A score grid corrects for the extra 0–0 and 1–1 draws a simpler model misses.

League 2 markets only offer Over lines, so League 2 shows no Under calls and no under-3 corners.
